4 · OECD 6-Step Due-Diligence Maturity

Score current practice against the six-step process in the OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct (2018). Scale: 0 = absent; 1 = ad-hoc; 2 = policy in place; 3 = operationalised at HQ / own ops; 4 = extends to T1 supply chain; 5 = extends through upstream and downstream value chain with public reporting and independent assurance.

Why the 0-5 ladder matters. CSDDD Article 8 embeds steps 2-3; Article 10 integrates step 3; Article 11 requires remediation (step 6); Articles 13+15 require monitoring (step 4) and public reporting (step 5). A gap at any step creates a directly actionable compliance risk under CSDDD and equivalent civil-liability regimes (Article 29).

5 · Grievance & Remedy — UNGP Principle 31

UNGP Principle 31 sets eight effectiveness criteria for non-judicial grievance mechanisms. CSDDD Article 14 operationalises this (notification procedure) and Article 15 sets remedy obligations when the company causes or contributes to an adverse impact. Score each criterion 0-5; target 4+ per criterion for "fit-for-purpose" status per Shift's Access to Remedy guidance (2021).

6 · CSDDD Obligation-by-Obligation Gap

Directive (EU) 2024/1760 — transposition by 26 July 2026; first application 26 July 2027 for largest in-scope companies; phased through July 2029. Article 4 imposes duty of care; Articles 5-16 specify operational duties; Article 22 requires a climate transition plan; Article 29 creates civil liability. Score each obligation 0-3 (0 = not started; 3 = fully compliant and evidenced).

Article 29 civil liability. Member states must provide for liability where a company intentionally or negligently fails to comply with Articles 10-11 obligations and the failure causes damage to a natural or legal person. Trade unions and NGOs may act in the name of victims. Limitation period ≥ 5 years.
